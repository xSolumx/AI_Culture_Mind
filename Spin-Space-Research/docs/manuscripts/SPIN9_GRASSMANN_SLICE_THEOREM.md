## Abstract

The Spin(9) action on \(G_3(\mathbb R^{16})\) has cohomogeneity three, but the
algebraic sensor curve lies on a singular orbit with a three-dimensional
\(\operatorname{SO}(3)\) stabilizer. At the Cayley-null plane, the orbit has
dimension \(33\), so its normal slice is six-dimensional. This note proves the
exact stabilizer-module decomposition

\[
N\cong V_1\oplus V_5.
\]

The trivial line is exactly tangent to the known one-parameter curve. The
five-dimensional summand is the spin-two representation, canonically
isomorphic to traceless symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices. Its invariant ring is
generated by \(\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(A^3)\).
Consequently the complete local quotient has the three natural coordinates

\[
c,\qquad p_2=\operatorname{tr}(A^2),
\qquad p_3=\operatorname{tr}(A^3).
\]

This is the exact local bridge from the solved one-variable determinant to the
full cohomogeneity-three orthonormal problem. The Hessian at the algebraic
curve optimum is completed in the companion
[strict local D-optimality theorem](SPIN9_STRICT_LOCAL_D_OPTIMALITY.md). The
present slice theorem is not a global parametrization of the Grassmann
quotient.

## 1. The six-dimensional slice

Let \(B\in\mathbb R^{16\times3}\) be the orthonormal Cayley-null frame and
\(P=BB^{\mathsf T}\). The Grassmann tangent space is

\[
T_WG_3(\mathbb R^{16})
\cong\{Z\in\mathbb R^{16\times3}:B^{\mathsf T}Z=0\},
\]

of dimension \(39\). The infinitesimal Spin(9) orbit is

\[
Z_X=(I-P)XB,
\qquad X\in\mathfrak{spin}(9).
\]

Exact row reduction gives orbit rank \(33\). Orthogonal intersection with the
horizontal tangent constraints therefore leaves a six-dimensional normal
slice.

## 2. Casimir certificate

The three exact stabilizer generators act on a horizontal variation through

\[
Z\longmapsto HZ-ZA_H,
\qquad A_H=B^{\mathsf T}HB.
\]

The induced six-dimensional matrices are skew with respect to the exact slice
metric. Their normalized Casimir has characteristic polynomial

\[
\chi_{\mathcal C}(\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda-6)^5.
\]

The zero eigenspace is one-dimensional and trivial. The eigenvalue six is the
Casimir value \(2(2+1)\), so its five-dimensional eigenspace is \(V_5\).
Hence

\[
\boxed{N\cong V_1\oplus V_5.}
\]

Differentiating the exact curve projector with respect to
\(u^2=(1-c)/(1+c)\) at \(u=1\) produces a nonzero slice vector annihilated by
the Casimir. Thus the known curve supplies precisely the trivial line.

## 3. The invariant coordinates

The real spin-two module is

\[
V_5\cong\operatorname{Sym}_0(3),
\]

with \(\operatorname{SO}(3)\) acting by conjugation. A traceless symmetric
\(3\times3\) matrix is determined up to orthogonal conjugacy by its unordered
eigenvalues, whose sum is zero. The invariant polynomial algebra is therefore

\[
\mathbb R[V_5]^{\operatorname{SO}(3)}
=\mathbb R[p_2,p_3],
\qquad
p_2=\operatorname{tr}(A^2),
\quad p_3=\operatorname{tr}(A^3).
\]

Together with the trivial coordinate \(c\), these give three local quotient
coordinates, matching the known cohomogeneity.

The feasible eigenvalue region obeys the discriminant inequality

\[
p_2^3-6p_3^2\ge0,
\]

with equality when two eigenvalues coincide. This semialgebraic cusp is the
natural domain for the next exact determinant reconstruction.

## 4. What this changes

The orthonormal gate is no longer an unspecified search over 39 tangent
coordinates. Locally, it is an invariant problem in three scalars.
The solved curve is the axis \(p_2=p_3=0\). The next exact question is whether

\[
\det I(c,p_2,p_3)
\le \det I(c_\star,0,0)
\]

throughout a global fundamental domain. At \(c=c_\star\), the companion exact
Hessian theorem proves strict local maximality on this slice and after coupling
it to the supported frame-spectrum \(V_5\). What remains here is global rather
than infinitesimal.

The theorem does not claim that \((c,p_2,p_3)\) is a single global coordinate
chart on all of \(G_3(\mathbb R^{16})/\operatorname{Spin}(9)\). Kollross's
nonpolarity result is an explicit warning against that shortcut.
