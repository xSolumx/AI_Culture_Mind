## Why a group appears only after the operator lift

The nonzero unit octonions are not an associative group. Their multiplication
is a Moufang loop. The maintained scan avoids this obstruction by replacing a
unit \(x\) by its real left-multiplication operator

\[
L_x:y\longmapsto xy.
\]

Although \(L_xL_y\) is generally not \(L_{xy}\), ordinary matrix composition is
associative. For the seven imaginary basis units, every \(L_{e_i}\) is an exact
integral signed-permutation matrix. This makes their finite operator closure a
well-defined group suitable for a parallel prefix scan.

## Result 1: the left-operator group

Let

\[
E=\langle L_{e_1},\ldots,L_{e_7}\rangle\leq SO(8).
\]

Exact closure gives

\[
|E|=128,
\qquad Z(E)=E'=\{I,-I\},
\qquad E/Z(E)\cong C_2^6.
\]

Every square and commutator is central. Exactly 72 elements square to the
identity; equivalently the element-order distribution is

| order | elements |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 71 |
| 4 | 56 |

The 72-square count fixes the Arf invariant, so this is the plus-type
extraspecial group

\[
\boxed{E\cong2_+^{1+6}.}
\]

The seven generators satisfy the real Clifford relations

\[
L_{e_i}L_{e_j}+L_{e_j}L_{e_i}=-2\delta_{ij}I,
\]

and their ordered volume product is \(-I\). Thus the reduction from seven
formal Clifford generators to a 128-element group is not numerical aliasing;
it is the exact irreducible eight-dimensional Clifford quotient.

## Result 2: signed Fano-basis automorphisms

The source exhausts all \(7!\,2^7\) signed imaginary-basis maps, retaining
exactly those which preserve all 64 basis products. The resulting subgroup

\[
A\leq G_2
\]

has order 1,344. Its projection to unsigned Fano-plane permutations has image

\[
\operatorname{PSL}(2,7)\cong\operatorname{GL}(3,2)
\]

of order 168 and elementary-abelian kernel \(C_2^3\).

For one exact \((2,3,7)\) generating pair in the quotient, the source checks all
64 pairs of lifts. Every pair generates all 1,344 elements; no order-168
complement exists. Hence

\[
\boxed{A\cong2^3.\operatorname{PSL}(2,7)}
\]

is the non-split extension. This agrees with the finite \(G_2\) subgroup
described by Koca, Koc, and Koca in
[Two groups of order 1344](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06107).

## Result 3: the generated operator normalizer

The two groups meet nontrivially:

\[
|E\cap A|=8.
\]

The intersection is precisely why multiplying the two orders would overcount.
Every element of \(A\) normalizes \(E\), and exact closure gives

\[
N=\langle E,A\rangle,
\qquad
|N|=\frac{128\cdot1344}{8}=21{,}504.
\]

The normal subgroup and quotient are

\[
E\triangleleft N,
\qquad N/E\cong\operatorname{PSL}(2,7).
\]

This extension does split. Among the 24 order-two and 16 order-three lifts of
one quotient generating pair, the exhaustive relator search finds an explicit
order-168 complement. Therefore

\[
\boxed{N\cong2_+^{1+6}:\operatorname{PSL}(2,7).}
\]

Further exact invariants are:

- \(Z(N)=\{I,-I\}\);
- \(N'=N\), so \(N\) is perfect and nonsolvable;
- all 21,504 matrices have determinant \(+1\);
- the action is transitive on the sixteen signed basis units
  \(\{\pm e_0,\ldots,\pm e_7\}\).

The GAP Character Table Library lists the known group
\(2_+^{1+6}.L_3(2)\) at order 21,504 in its
[table-by-order index](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/homes/Thomas.Breuer/ctbllib/ctbltoc/views/allbyorder.html).
The present calculation therefore does **not** discover a new abstract finite
group. It identifies the repository's concrete monomial representation with
that standard extension structure, including an explicit split complement.

## What is newly learned here

Before this calculation, the repository used the Fano table to construct
Clifford generators and an associative operator scan, but did not identify the
finite group generated by its discrete basis transitions. The exact result now
separates three objects that were easy to conflate:

1. the nonassociative octonion unit loop;
2. the plus-extraspecial associative left-operator group \(E\); and
3. its perfect 21,504-element Fano-symmetry extension \(N\).

This supplies a substantially harder finite-state benchmark than \(Q_8\),
\(A_5\), or \(2.A_5\). It has a center-sensitive extraspecial normal subgroup
and a non-solvable simple quotient in one faithful eight-dimensional signed-
permutation action. A future prefix-product task can therefore test whether a
model tracks both the central Clifford cocycle and the quotient action without
requiring dense floating-point targets.

external nomenclature comparison, not for acceptance.

For the combined extension, the split diagnostic exhausts all
\(24\times16=384\) order-compatible lift pairs. Exactly 192 have product
order seven and 64 also have commutator order four; the stored certificate
includes one explicit pair whose closure is an order-168 complement.

The broader mathematical relationship between Moufang loops and groups with
triality is established theory; see the
