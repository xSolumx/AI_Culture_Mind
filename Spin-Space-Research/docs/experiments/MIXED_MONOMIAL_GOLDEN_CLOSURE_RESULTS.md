## Question

Let

\[
N\cong2_+^{1+6}:\operatorname{PSL}(2,7),
\qquad |N|=21{,}504,
\]

be the exact signed-monomial group generated by the seven maintained left
octonion operators and the two maintained signed-Fano generators. Separately,
the fixed binary-icosahedral embedding supplies an $A_5$ vector image and two
faithful $2.A_5$ half-spin images in the same eight-dimensional basis. Is the
group generated by $N$ and any of those golden-field images finite?

The answer is no for all three views. A single word of length three proves
infinitude, and the exact Clifford-adjoint decomposition then proves that each
group is dense in $SO(8)$.

## Arithmetic infinite-order proof

Put

\[
c=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{4}.
\]

The exact characteristic polynomials are

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_v(x)=p_-(x)
={}&x^8+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}x^7+c x^6+c x^2
+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}x+1,
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_+(x)={}&x^8-x^7+c x^6+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}x^5
+\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}x^4\\
&+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}x^3+c x^2-x+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The integer ring of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ is

\[
\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)}
=\mathbb Z\left[\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right].
\]

The coefficient $c$ is not in this ring. Equivalently, its field trace and
norm are $1/2$ and $-1/4$, so its monic quadratic polynomial over
$\mathbb Q$ does not have integer coefficients.

If $T_\rho$ had finite order, all its eigenvalues would be roots of unity.
Every characteristic coefficient would then be an algebraic integer, because
it is an elementary symmetric polynomial in algebraic integers. The displayed
coefficient $c$ contradicts that implication. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(T_v)
=\operatorname{ord}(T_+)
=\operatorname{ord}(T_-)=\infty.}
\]

The artifact also computes the rational characteristic norm

\[
\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)/\mathbb Q}(p_\rho)
=p_\rho\,p_\rho^\sigma.
\]

Its coefficient denominators are exactly $\{1,2,4\}$. A monic polynomial
whose roots are all roots of unity is a product of cyclotomic polynomials and
lies in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Thus the nonintegral norm supplies a second exact form
of the same non-cyclotomic obstruction.

## Exact adjoint-density proof

Let $L_1,\ldots,L_7$ be the seven maintained skew octonion left operators and
define

\[
V_7=\operatorname{span}\{L_i\},
\qquad
V_{21}=\operatorname{span}\{L_iL_j:i<j\}.
\]

Exact Frobenius ranks and inner products give the orthogonal splitting

\[
\mathfrak{so}(8)=V_7\oplus V_{21},
\qquad \dim V_7=7,
\qquad \dim V_{21}=21.
\]

Conjugation by every maintained monomial generator preserves both summands.
The exact centralizer systems for the induced representations have the
following dimensions:

| Representation | Linear system | Rank | Commutant dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $V_7$ | $441\times49$ | 48 | 1 |
| $V_{21}$ | $3969\times441$ | 440 | 1 |
| $V_7\oplus V_{21}$ | $7056\times784$ | 782 | 2 |

Because these are orthogonal real representations, a proper invariant
subspace would supply a non-scalar orthogonal projector in the commutant.
Therefore $V_7$ and $V_{21}$ are irreducible and inequivalent, and the only
monomial-invariant subspaces of $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ are

\[
0,\quad V_7,\quad V_{21},\quad\mathfrak{so}(8).
\]

The Clifford brackets satisfy

\[
[V_7,V_7]=V_{21},
\]

with exact bracket-span dimension 21, so $V_7$ is not a Lie subalgebra.
Conversely, an exhaustive exact bracket check proves

\[
[V_{21},V_{21}]\subseteq V_{21};
\]

this is the proper $\mathfrak{spin}(7)$ candidate.

That final candidate is destroyed by the golden generator. Conjugating the
21 basis bivectors by $b$ and projecting onto $V_7$ gives 33 nonzero
coefficients in the vector view and 12 in each half-spin view. Already the
projection of $L_1L_2$ onto $L_1$ is

\[
-\frac14,\qquad +\frac12,\qquad -\frac12
\]

in the vector, positive-half-spin, and negative-half-spin views respectively.
Thus no golden $b$ normalizes $V_{21}$.

For any view $\rho$, let $K_\rho$ be the compact closure of the generated
group. The infinite-order witness proves that $K_\rho$ is infinite. A
zero-dimensional compact Lie group is finite, so the identity Lie algebra
$\mathfrak k_\rho$ is nonzero. It is invariant under both the monomial group
and $\rho(b)$. The invariant-subspace classification, bracket closure, and
normalizer failure leave only

\[
\mathfrak k_\rho=\mathfrak{so}(8).
\]

Consequently the identity component is $SO(8)$ itself, and hence

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\langle N,\rho(2.A_5)\rangle}=SO(8)
}
\]

for either faithful half-spin view; the same statement holds with the vector
$A_5$ image.

## What this settles

For the repository's fixed relative basis conventions, this proves:

- the mixed monomial/vector-$A_5$ group is infinite;
- both mixed monomial/$2.A_5$ half-spin groups are infinite;
- all three groups are topologically dense in $SO(8)$;
- the finite order-864,000 common-carrier triality closure does not remain
  finite after adjoining the monomial operator group;
- no finite faithful action on a spanning 240-point $E_8$ root configuration
  can realize any of these exact mixed closures; and
- the failure is visible at word length three even though every mixed word of
  length two remains finite.

This is a particularly useful negative result: extensive cyclotomic behaviour
at the generator-pair layer was real, but it did not extend to the generated
group.

## Claim boundary

The result proves topological density for these three fixed relative
embeddings. It does not provide a quantitative spectral gap, word-length
equidistribution rate, or random-walk mixing theorem. It does not classify
every relative embedding of the two abstract finite groups, identify a new
abstract infinite group, or demonstrate an ML-quality or kernel-speed
advantage. The separate
[low-degree mixing continuation](MIXED_MONOMIAL_GOLDEN_MIXING_RESULTS.md)
subsequently certifies the defining, adjoint, and traceless-symmetric bands;
it still does not prove a full `L2(SO(8))` gap.
