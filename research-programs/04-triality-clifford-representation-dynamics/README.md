# Programme 04: Triality and Clifford representation dynamics

## Scope

Claims that genuinely depend on the exceptional representation structure of
Spin(8) or Spin(9): the shared `8v`, `8+`, and `8-` actions, the triality
intertwiner, cross-view transport, Clifford bind/unbind maps, center and
quotient structure, and representation-specific memory controls.

## Core Questions

1. What exact bind, unbind, transport, and action-completion identities follow
   from the Spin(8) triality tensor or the Spin(9) Clifford system?
2. Which benefits survive direct, SO(3), generic orthogonal, and matched-state
   controls?
3. Can exceptional structure help routing or cross-view inference without
   being mistaken for additional storage capacity?

## Proven / Established Results

- One shared 28-dimensional bivector action generates the maintained vector
  and two chiral Spin(8) representations, with bracket, center-signature,
  equivariance, and norm-preservation checks in the theorem repository.
- Unit triality binding is an exact norm-preserving single-pair bind/unbind
  primitive under the maintained normalization.
- With supplied unit keys and shared transport, triality-coded addressed slots
  are an orthogonal change of gauge of same-width direct slots. They have the
  same hard-route capacity and retrieval error.
- Orthogonal multiplicity channels isolate at most the multiplicity dimension;
  raw superposition in one eight-dimensional triality space is not a
  high-capacity associative memory.
- The prospective Spin(8) paired-action cohort supports shared cross-view
  action completion. The identical direct/delta rows and the separate SO(3)
  cross-product intertwiner experiment locate the benefit in the
  representation prior, not the update law or exceptional storage. That SO(3)
  row is an experiment control, not a standalone model.
- Spin(9) supplies exact reversible `9 -> 16` Clifford binding and an
  equivariant nine-score Hopf coarse index. Current dynamic-slot tests reduce
  to a direct-memory gauge.
- The adjacent seven Spin(8) coordinate rotations close exactly to dimension
  28. The vector and two chiral eight-dimensional modules each have real Schur
  type, have zero cross-Hom spaces, and retain that separation after independent
  dense \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) conjugacies. The Spin(9) spin module restricts
  through exact chirality projectors of ranks eight and eight.
- Inside the existing real Spin(9) Clifford system, setting
  \(e_0=P_0\) and \(e_i=P_0P_i\) for \(1\leq i\leq4\) gives a faithful
  16-dimensional image of \(\mathrm{Cl}(1,4)\): one generator squares to
  \(+I\), four square to \(-I\), and all anticommute. Its central volume
  involution splits the algebra and module into two quaternionic sectors.
- The positive generators \(c_i=e_0e_i=P_i\), \(1\leq i\leq3\), give the
  exact inclusion \(\mathrm{Cl}(3,0)\hookrightarrow
  \mathrm{Cl}^0(1,4)\). On the full 16-dimensional module this restricted
  action is four copies of the four-dimensional complex-type real irreducible.
  This algebraic restriction is distinct from the maintained model's
  conjugation decomposition `2V0 + 2V1`.

## Open Claims

- Learning the Spin(8) action/frame from raw data rather than supplying it.
- A parameter- and state-matched Spin(9) cross-chiral routing advantage over
  generic `O(16)` and direct controls.
- Naturalistic cross-view tasks on which exceptional representation structure
  improves sample efficiency or generalization.
- Any model-level advantage attributable specifically to triality or Clifford
  structure.

## Non-claims

- Exact single-pair inversion is not high-capacity retrieval.
- Width-expanding `9 -> 16` binding is not a same-state capacity advantage.
- A supplied equivariant frame is not learned representation discovery.
- Spin(8) examples do not make the general scan, prior, or memory laws
  triality-specific.
