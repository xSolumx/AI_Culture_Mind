# Programme 05: Information geometry and Dirac--Gram sensing

## Scope

Exact and computer-assisted theorems about identifying shared Spin actions from
probe frames, conditioning their information operators, and comparing
correlated frames through Gram-normalized determinant inequalities. Spin(8)
sensing, Spin(8) Dirac--Gram positivity, and Spin(9) sensing remain separate
claim families inside this theorem programme.

## Core Questions

1. How many probes identify a generic shared action, and what stabilizers remain
   on exceptional allocations?
2. Which balanced frames optimize information criteria locally, within a
   canonical family, or globally?
3. Does the strengthened Dirac--Gram inequality hold on the full feasible
   correlated-frame domain?
4. Which conclusions are human proofs, computer-assisted finite certificates,
   exact reductions, or numerical falsification evidence?

## Proven / Established Results

- Spin(8) mixed-probe work establishes the allocation-specific four-versus-five
  generic stabilizer law, exact balanced Cayley spectra, and strict local
  equal-five-query results under their stated quotient/domain contracts.
- On the orthonormal balanced Spin(8) family, the Cayley-null construction has
  the recorded exact determinant and trace invariants and the stated
  conditioning optima.
- The Spin(8) Dirac--Gram ledger contains computer-assisted theorems on named
  constrained families, including signed-star, variable-Cayley one-edge, and
  frozen two-edge sectors, plus exact unrestricted reconstruction and boundary
  reductions. These results retain their individual domains.
- On the adjacent endpoint-octet five-cube, the complete determinant
  coordinate boundary is now exact: nine faces collapse to one-mode perfect
  squares and the tenth is the hash-bound identity \(Z=X^2\). The determinant
  with all five coordinates strictly interior remains open.
- On the disjoint central core \([1/4,3/4]^5\), an exact 32-box Bernstein
  certificate proves the stronger Walsh diagonal-dominance inequality, so all
  unique physical margins are strictly positive. An adaptive continuation
  expands this to \([1/8,7/8]^5\) with 2,140 exact leaves and no unresolved
  box. Only the width-\(1/8\) collars between that extended core and the
  coordinate boundary remain uncertified.
- The Spin(9) Clifford-sensing ledger establishes exact generic
  identifiability, frame-operator reduction, and a strict local D-optimality
  theorem modulo Spin(9) for the displayed rank-three candidate. A separate
  float64 autodiff path reproduces the expected quotient signature as
  falsification evidence, not as an additional exact proof.
- The concrete Cayley-null Grassmann stabilizer matrices are now linked
  exactly to the abstract quotient decomposition: a full
  \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) intertwiner of determinant \(-1/4\) sends the slice to
  \(V_1\oplus V_5\), sends the known curve tangent to the canonical trivial
  coordinate, and combines with the supported coefficient module to give the
  compiler-certified \(V_1\oplus2V_5\) representation. This strengthens the
  representation interface, not the finite-radius determinant bound.
- Independently of that rationalizing coordinate bridge, the declared
  \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) compiler now consumes the native algebraic slice and
  full-quotient matrices directly and recovers (V_1\oplus V_5) and
  (V_1\oplus2V_5). This closes a coefficient-field tooling gap; it supplies
  no sign information for the remaining determinant numerator.
- The finite-radius Spin(9) Grassmann certificate now controls the complete
  pure spin-two graph family over the Cayley-null plane. A deterministic
  characteristic-zero lift and six-cell exact Bernstein atlas prove a strict
  \(101/100\) upper bound, so the symmetric algebraic candidate beats every
  such graph. The axisymmetric boundary still supplies an exact counterexample
  to the tempting stronger claim that Cayley-null maximizes each radial ray.
- The coupled \(V_1\oplus V_5\) layer now has an exact characteristic-zero
  identity and a global \(21/20\) determinant bound on its complete
  finite-radius graph slice. The proof combines a 312-leaf compact atlas,
  eight exact handoff boxes, eight strict local charts, and a 22-prime graded
  raw replay under both embeddings of \(\sqrt2\). The 175-digit prime product
  exceeds twice an explicit residual coefficient bound. This is global on the
  stated slice, not exact candidate optimality or the unrestricted quotient.

## Open Claims

- The global optimum among exactly five equal Spin(8) observations remains
  open; local optimality and the approximate-design relaxation do not settle
  it.
- The unrestricted Spin(8) Dirac--Gram inequality remains open on the complete
  feasible seven-variable domain; even the adjacent endpoint-octet reduction
  still has an uncertified all-five-coordinate strict interior.
- The global exact three-spinor Spin(9) optimum remains open; the maintained
  theorem is strict local optimality modulo symmetry plus a complete pure-
  \(V_5\) graph theorem and a complete \(21/20\) bound on the first coupled
  \(V_1\oplus V_5\) graph slice. Exact maximality of the algebraic candidate
  even on that slice, the second supported \(V_5\), and the global quotient
  remain uncertified.
- External independent review of the computer-assisted certificates remains a
  publication gate.

## Dependencies

- The three canonical evidence ledgers below define their own domains, artifacts,
  replay commands, and trust boundaries.
- Exact reconstruction, interpolation bounds, positivity certificates, and
  numerical screens are different evidence layers and must remain separate.
- No memory, scanner, rotor-SSM, or Collatz claim depends on these global
  sensing theorems unless an explicit bridge is proved later.

## Non-claims

- A constrained-family theorem is not the unrestricted theorem.
- A numerical search, successful holdout, or artifact checksum is not a global
  positivity proof.
- A sensing optimum is not a memory-capacity or sequence-model result.
- Spin(9) local optimality does not exclude distant asymmetric or singular
  competitors.
