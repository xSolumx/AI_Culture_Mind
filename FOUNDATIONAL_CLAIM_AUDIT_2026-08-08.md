## Executive verdict

The maintained archive does not support a single claim that
"Spin geometry solves sequence modelling." It supports several narrower and
separable results. The strongest exact mathematics survives this audit, and
the Spin(9) strict-local theorem survives an additional independent Hessian-
path calculation on a 44-dimensional chart. Four concrete weaknesses were
nevertheless found and corrected:

1. a formal degree-growth calculation had been described too broadly as an
   obstruction to generic cyclic scan algorithms;
2. a tight-frame coefficient-energy bound had been allowed to read like a
   deterministic retrieval-error bound for every possible collection of
   stored values;
3. one evaluation path averaged batch means instead of weighting every target
   equally; and
4. two committed unrestricted-Dirac summary files had stale entries in the
   artifact manifest.

These corrections narrow claims; they do not manufacture a stronger result.
The global Spin(8) equal-five-query problem, unrestricted Spin(8) Dirac--Gram
inequality, global Spin(9) three-spinor design problem, and any
triality-specific language-model advantage remain open.

## Adversarial method

Each maintained headline was attacked through six possible failure modes.

1. **Definition substitution:** is a familiar theorem being applied to a
   different object with the same informal name?
2. **Domain enlargement:** was a result on a chart, orbit, allocation, or
   constrained family promoted to the unrestricted domain?
3. **Evidence substitution:** was a numerical screen, interpolation, or
   artifact checksum presented as a proof of a mathematical implication?
4. **Gauge contamination:** were symmetry directions mistaken for physical
   curvature or identifiability?
5. **Boundary blindness:** could an interior result fail at singular or distant
   configurations?
6. **Novelty inflation:** was a known algebraic ingredient presented as the
   new result rather than the particular theorem or architecture built from
   it?

Historical reports remain available as a record of what was believed at each
stage. They are not equal in authority to the current program ledgers,
correction notes, theorem manuscripts, executable certificates, and complete
artifacts.

## Program-by-program verdict

### 1. Selective rotor state-space models

**Survives.** The maintained recurrence is an affine, algebraically
associative scan with Spin(3)-equivariant rotor transport and an explicit
finite-input state bound. The same-position prefix-product target contract is
correct; this is a state-tracking task, not next-token prediction.

**Does not survive as a broader claim.** Exact associativity does not imply
bitwise floating-point equality. The current controlled experiments establish
that transport is causally active, but not that rotors provide a memory,
compute, or general language-modelling advantage over matched Householder,
delta-rule, or discrete-transition baselines.

### 2. Shared-family representation learning

**Survives.** Joint constraints can eliminate relational nullspaces that
independent token-wise fitting leaves unobserved. The exact rank counts and
held-out completions are controlled identifiability results.

**Boundary.** They do not show semantic discovery from natural data, and a
shared-family retraction is not uniquely Spin(8). The Birkhoff address result,
for example, is a relational permutation-completion mechanism.

### 3. Triality memory and Intertwiner SchurScan

**Survives.** A triangular acyclic bilinear recurrence has a finite homogeneous
lift and therefore an exact associative staged scan. The triality tensor is an
exceptional equivariant instance, not the only instance.

**Corrected.** For the two polynomial feedback graphs actually analysed, the
generic degrees obey

\[
d_{t+1}=d_t+1
\quad\text{or}\quad
d_{t+1}=2d_t.
\]

This rules out a fixed finite **monomial linear lift** unless leading terms
cancel. It does not rule out every nonlinear coordinate system, special
closure identity, or associative algorithm for cyclic feedback.

**Memory boundary corrected.** Unit triality binding is an exact isometry for
one pair. Orthogonal multiplicity codes give exact linear retrieval for
\(K\leq H\). For \(K>H\), the Welch/frame-potential bound controls average
code-correlation energy. It equals expected retrieval mean-square error for
independent zero-mean isotropic stored values; it is not a pointwise lower
bound for every correlated value collection, whose cross-terms may cancel or
reinforce.

### 4. Spin(8) sensing and Cayley design

**Survives.** The allocation-specific four-versus-five generic stabilizer law,
the exact balanced Cayley spectrum, and strict local equal-five-query optimum
modulo Spin(8) remain supported by their stated exact contracts.

The global normal form uses a real external theorem: Spin(7) acts with
cohomogeneity one on the oriented four-plane Grassmannian, with Cayley and
oppositely oriented Cayley singular orbits. The repository-specific
two-plus-two split reduction is checked separately. The external
classification and the local exact calculation must not be conflated.

**Open.** A strict local optimum, a finite coordinate-circle atlas, and the
global optimum in the approximate-design relaxation do not prove the global
optimum among exactly five equal observations.

### 5. Spin(8) Dirac--Gram inequalities

**Survives within stated domains.** The signed-star theorem and completed edge
and frozen-sector certificates are computer-assisted theorems on their named
families. Exact reconstruction, divisibility, and Bernstein or matrix
positivity obligations are separate and replayable.

**Open.** None of those certificates proves the unrestricted Gram--Cayley
inequality. Numerical interior searches and boundary sensitivity maps are
falsifiers, not absence-of-counterexample proofs.

### 6. Spin(9) three-spinor sensing

**Survives as a strict local theorem.** The candidate frame lies in the
rank-three, trace-three frame-operator stratum. Schur--Horn supplies the
converse realization by three unit spinors; this is not an approximate-design
relaxation. The exact certificate accounts for 33 orbit directions, one curve
direction, and two coupled five-dimensional slice modules.

The previously identified gauge risk was attacked through an independent
Hessian path. A fresh PyTorch float64 calculation differentiates the direct
36-by-36 information determinant on a 44-coordinate rank-three chart without
importing the exact Hessian certificate or its quotient projection. It shares
the foundational Spin(9) generator constructor, so it is not an independent
implementation of the base Clifford system. It finds 11 negative directions,
33 numerical symmetry zeros, and no positive direction. This strongly
falsifies sign, dimension, and hidden-gauge mistakes while remaining numerical
evidence rather than part of the exact proof.

The exact statement is still only **strict local D-optimality modulo Spin(9)**
at the displayed algebraic point. It does not exclude distant asymmetric
maxima or singular-boundary competitors.

### 7. Benchmarks and the historical prototype

Only complete structured artifacts under a frozen protocol count as benchmark
results. Smoke tests, interrupted runs, out-of-memory traces, and profiler
hypotheses remain engineering evidence. The original SpinorModel is a
historical prototype; its componentwise nonlinearities and normalization do
not satisfy the maintained equivariance contract.

## Literature cross-check

All 36 distinct arXiv identifiers cited by link or bibliography entry in the
maintained archive were resolved against the arXiv metadata service. The titles
match the local bibliography, including the recent 2026 SSM references.

Several primary sources materially constrain the claims:

- Berndt and Tamaru provide the cohomogeneity-one Spin(7) action used in the
  Cayley normal-form bridge.
- Katz and Shnider prove the Cayley form's comass and Spin(7) stabilizer.
- Hiratani and Sompolinsky already study optimal quadratic and octonionic
  binding, so octonionic multiplication alone is not a novelty claim.
- Modern diagonal-SSM limits, DeltaProduct, discrete transition models,
  separated erase/write memories, and nonlinear matrix recurrences are
  mandatory baselines; none proves rotor or triality uniqueness.

The literature audit checks positioning and imported premises. It is not
external peer review of the repository's novel proofs.

## Remaining trust boundaries and nonclaims

1. No local self-audit substitutes for an independent referee reconstructing
   the proof from the compact certificate.
2. Passing hashes prove byte integrity, not the mathematical implication from
   those bytes.
3. Interpolation identities require an independent degree bound or symbolic
   derivation before they become theorems.
4. CUDA and Monte Carlo sweeps can find counterexamples but cannot certify
   positivity on a continuum.
5. Strict local optimality is not global optimality.
6. Equivariance and scan compatibility do not imply empirical usefulness.
7. A known exceptional algebra does not make every architecture built from it
   novel or superior.

## Publication-safe bottom line

The archive now contains several defensible papers rather than one universal
story. The most mature mathematical claims are constrained, exact sensing and
determinant theorems; the most mature architectural claim is the finite-lift
triangular bilinear scan. The correct next research gates are global
counterexample exclusion for the Spin(9) and equal-five-query design problems,
completion or falsification of unrestricted Dirac--Gram positivity, and
matched task benchmarks for the scan architectures. None should borrow proof
strength from another program.
